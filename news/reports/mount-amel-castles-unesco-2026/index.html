Five Mount Amel Castles Receive UNESCO World Heritage Recognition

South Lebanon · Published 6 November 2026 · Source check: 6 November 2026

On 26 July 2026, UNESCO announced the emergency inscription of five historic fortified sites in South Lebanon on the World Heritage List, while also placing the property on the List of World Heritage in Danger.

Five sites, one historical landscape

The inscription covers Qalaat al-Chakif (Beaufort), Qalaat Tibnin (Toron), Qalaat Chakra (Dubieh), Qalaat Deir Kifa (Maron) and Qalaat Chama’.

UNESCO describes the castles as a connected defensive and administrative landscape. Developed across centuries, their fortifications reflect changes in architecture, territorial control and construction techniques from the medieval period into the nineteenth century.

Why emergency inscription?

UNESCO’s announcement explained that emergency procedures respond to major threats affecting heritage. Armed conflict and serious risks to cultural sites are among the reasons for urgent protection.

Being recognized internationally does not mean the castles are safe, conserved or currently open to visitors. They were simultaneously added to the World Heritage in Danger list.

What follows

The decision creates an international record of the property’s cultural importance and highlights the need for documentation, protection and conservation. This is a July 2026 heritage development, not an announcement of any November opening or access arrangements.
