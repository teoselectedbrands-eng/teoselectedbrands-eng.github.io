Tripoli’s Rachid Karami Fair: Progress, Protection and an Unfinished Future

Tripoli · Published 6 November 2026 · Source check: 6 November 2026

The Rachid Karami International Fair is one of Lebanon’s major modernist architectural landmarks. Its conservation is still a live question, despite new steps welcomed by UNESCO in 2026.

A landmark and a decision

Designed by Brazilian architect Oscar Niemeyer, the complex entered UNESCO’s World Heritage List in 2023, while simultaneously joining the List of World Heritage in Danger.

At its 48th session in 2026, UNESCO’s World Heritage Committee welcomed designation of the fair as National Heritage, appointment of a new board and reported actions involving institutional assessment, archives, management planning and conservation.

Recognition is not restoration

The Committee did not declare the site fully restored. It requested further protection and conservation measures, along with management and land-use plans, and called for an updated state-of-conservation report by 1 February 2027.

The property remains on the List of World Heritage in Danger. The difference between actions reported to the Committee and work independently verified on site is important.

Why the decision matters

The fair links architecture, urban planning, cultural memory and the future of an unfinished public landmark in Tripoli. Its story is not simply about an individual building: it is also about how cities care for ambitious shared spaces over generations.

Source dates and limitations

This report summarizes UNESCO’s 2026 decision; it does not certify completed conservation work or provide visitor-access advice. Decision year: 2026. Sources checked 6 November 2026.
