Antarctic ozone hole ranks seventh by average area

Aetherion News · Source published 4 November 2026

NASA and NOAA reported on 4 November 2026 that the Antarctic ozone hole averaged about 25 million square kilometres from 7 September to 13 October. It was the seventh-largest seasonal average in 48 years of monitoring. The daily peak reached 27.1 million square kilometres on 18 September.

The agencies linked the large area to an unusually strong polar vortex, a ring of winds that keeps cold air over Antarctica. Ozone in the upper atmosphere helps shield life from ultraviolet radiation. NASA and NOAA report that long-term ozone recovery continues under international limits on ozone-depleting chemicals.
