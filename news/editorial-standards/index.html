Our editorial standards.

These proposed rules explain how Aetherion News will check evidence, assign responsibility and correct errors. They are being reviewed before any News reports are published.

Publication authority: the owner approves each publication. Writers and reviewers may contribute under accurate, agreed credits.

What belongs in News

Aetherion News is being prepared for timely, factual reporting across science, technology, society, culture, health, the environment, education and sport. A report must identify a substantive development and explain why it matters, using evidence appropriate to the claim.

News is separate from the Journal, which contains essays and clearly labeled creative companions, and from Aetherion Updates, which carries official organizational notices. Demonstrations, imagined events and fictional scenarios do not enter the News feed.

Editorial responsibility

Aetherion News is the public publication credit. The project owner retains final publication authority. Each report and each public update, correction, retraction or policy change requires the owner’s explicit approval before release. Preparation, editing or review does not confer publication authority.

A preparer and an editorial reviewer are assigned for each report. The reviewer checks the sources and records the evidence, limits and review outcome. The owner then approves or holds the specific version proposed for publication. If one person fills several roles, the record must say so and must not imply independent review. Obtain appropriate additional expertise or hold a consequential claim when the reviewer cannot assess it adequately.

Public credits describe the work actually done. A writer credit identifies the writer; an optional edited-by or reviewed-by credit identifies that contributor. The credit format may use Aetherion News alone or add accurate, agreed contributor names. Internal records identify the preparer, reviewer and owner’s decision. A contributor credit does not transfer final approval authority.

Original sources and traceable claims

Support material claims with accessible original records when available: research papers and underlying methods, official documents, datasets, direct statements or other first-hand evidence. Record the source URL, issuer or author, publication or update date, access date and the specific claim it supports.

Read the underlying record rather than relying on a headline, search excerpt, AI summary or press release alone. Attribute reporting obtained from another newsroom and follow it to its original evidence where possible. If an important original record cannot be inspected, explain that limitation and narrow or withhold the affected claim.

Check quotations against the original passage and preserve their context. Identify translated quotations and the original language when relevant. Credit images, charts and borrowed material; permission to use an asset and the evidence supporting its contents are separate checks.

Corroboration and disputed information

Seek independent corroboration for central claims when it is available, especially when a claim is contested, unexpected or consequential. Reprints of the same release, syndicated copies and accounts that depend on one original statement are not independent confirmations.

A single authenticated primary record can establish a bounded fact about that record, such as what an institution announced. It does not by itself establish every assertion in the announcement. Identify single-source reporting and any unresolved uncertainty. If a material disputed claim cannot be checked adequately, hold it rather than turn repetition into certainty.

When reporting a serious criticism of a person or organization, seek a meaningful response and describe the response or the relevant attempt to obtain one. Do not present the existence of an allegation as proof that the allegation is true.

Dates and publication history

Keep four dates distinct where applicable: when the event happened, when the supporting source was published or updated, when Aetherion published its report, and when the report was materially changed. The internal record also retains when the source was accessed.

If an event date is unknown, say so. Identify the period covered by a dataset and the time zone when timing could affect interpretation. A newly published source about an older event must not make the event appear new. Follow-ups explain what has changed since the earlier report.

Evidence limits and interpretation

State important limits beside the relevant claim. For research, describe the study type, population or sample, what was measured and the limits of applying the findings elsewhere. Identify preprints or other material that has not completed the review process being discussed.

Separate an observation from a causal conclusion, an estimate from a measured result, and a source-established fact from the editor’s interpretation. Keep denominators, units, time periods and meaningful uncertainty with numerical claims. Explain how conflicting evidence affects the conclusion.

The editorial angle may explain relevance or raise a question; it must not supply missing evidence. Health reporting must not turn a general finding into an individual diagnosis or treatment instruction.

Briefs explainers and follow-ups

A Brief states the development, relevant dates, original source and key limitation concisely. An Explainer adds context and explains what the evidence does and does not support. A Follow-up identifies new evidence, changes or corrections relative to a linked earlier report.

Every format keeps the same verification and approval requirements. A short report still needs clear attribution, relevant uncertainty and an accountable publication decision.

Corrections updates and retractions

A factual correction should be labeled as a correction, with a visible date and a concise account of what was wrong, what was changed and why. Place the note where readers of the affected report can find it. Review connected headlines, summaries, captions and other controlled copies when the error affects them.

Distinguish a correction from a development that happened after the original report and from routine spelling or formatting edits that do not change meaning. Material changes must remain understandable in the report’s history.

If a report cannot be supported, withdraw the unsupported material and leave an explanatory notice where appropriate. Retain a controlled internal revision record. A transparent correction notice need not repeat private or harmful details that should no longer be displayed. The owner approves a public correction or retraction before release.

Requesting a correction

Correction requests can be sent to hello@aetherionmovement.com, the general mailbox listed on the Aetherion website.

Correction contact: hello@aetherionmovement.com

Use the subject Aetherion News correction. Include the report URL, the passage or claim concerned, the reason for the request and a supporting public source when available. Describe the issue without sending medical records, confidential case files, credentials or identifiable information about children.

The assigned reviewer records the concern, evidence, proposed outcome and any proposed public correction. The owner approves the public response or content change before release. Review priority follows the significance and impact of the possible error.

Images illustrations and data graphics

Match documentary imagery to its actual source, context and date. Do not use a generic or altered image in a way that implies it records the event being reported. Identify archive imagery, illustration, reconstruction and generated artwork clearly.

Topic icons are navigation illustrations, not evidence of a reported event. Data graphics must be generated from identified data with appropriate labels, units and scale. A generated illustration must not impersonate documentary evidence or an authentic quotation.

AI assistance and human review

AI tools may assist preparation, organization, translation or editing, but their output is not a verified source. A human editor must check factual claims against the cited records and remains responsible for publication. Disclose a material role of generative AI in published text or imagery when relevant to a reader’s understanding.

Do not invent a quotation, source, event or witness account. Do not place private clinical, learner, worker or institutional records into a public reporting workflow. Arabic and French publication require competent human language review; an automated translation is not that review.

Affiliations and editorial independence

Disclose relevant financial, institutional or personal connections when they could affect a reader’s assessment of a report. Funding or commercial relationships do not determine the factual conclusion. Identify promotional material and official Aetherion announcements as such.

Aetherion Updates remains the destination for organizational notices. A report involving Aetherion or a related project requires an explicit affiliation disclosure and an editorial assessment of whether it belongs in News. A factual link between projects does not authorize sharing their private records.

The internal editorial record

Keep a versioned record of the report, material claims and sources, date checks, corroboration and limits, preparer and reviewer, relevant interests, rights and image checks, language review when needed, review findings and the owner’s approval decision. Link each approval to the exact version and retain later corrections. A material change after approval needs a new owner decision.

These records support accountability and may have restricted access. They are separate from public story pages. HavenLine client and workforce data, secure Learning records and Inquiry submissions do not form a public News source collection.

Draft status and publication conditions

This is an owner-review draft, version 0.3, prepared on 4 November 2026. No verified News reports have been published in this local candidate. It does not claim external newsroom certification or affiliation.

Publishing requires the owner’s approval of this policy, completed report-level preparation and review, approval of the exact report version and the separate technical and release approvals. The correction contact is recorded from the existing site and the owner’s confirmation that the mailbox is active and can receive messages. Draft pages remain noindex and outside the public sitemap until the relevant release decision. Policy and contributor credits do not activate a website or delegate the owner’s publication authority.
