Four ideas to begin with

Questions you could explore with a group. These are proposed educational themes, not advertised sessions or guaranteed outcomes.

LEARNING & INCLUSION

How can one lesson include different learners?

Compare a shared learning goal with several ways to participate. UNESCO's inclusive teaching framework emphasises planning for learner diversity and active participation.

UNESCO IBE resource pack · Background, not workshop evaluation

MOVEMENT & PARTICIPATION

Can movement be designed for more people?

Explore activity options and participation barriers. WHO describes evidence-based physical-activity recommendations for different ages and people living with disability.

WHO guidelines, 2020 · General guidance, not a class prescription

WORK & ERGONOMICS

What can we change about a work task?

Begin with the task, its demands and the people doing it. NIOSH and OSHA both describe worker participation and iterative improvement in ergonomics.

CDC/NIOSH ergonomics resources · Design context, not a site assessment

TECHNOLOGY & CRITICAL THINKING

How should educators question AI tools?

Consider human agency, ethics, applications and classroom use. UNESCO's framework identifies competency areas for teachers learning to work with AI.

UNESCO AI framework, 2024 · Source guidance, not course accreditation

Possible learning outcome: A movement-based classroom activity

Related reading

Explore the original activity concept: A Classroom That Moves

Choose a transition in a fictional school day: arriving, regrouping after a break or preparing for a shared task. Educators work from the actual constraints of a classroom to design an activity with a clear purpose and more than one way to participate.

  • Connect a brief activity with a learning or transition purpose.
  • Adapt space, noise, instructions and participation choices.
  • Produce a plan another educator can understand and review.
  • Select a classroom transition and draw the available space.
  • Compare two short activity prototypes.
  • Design one activity with a quieter or seated alternative.
  • Teach the plan to another educator and revise it using practical feedback.

AET-WS-004Schools & Education

Teaching Critical Thinking

Suitable for Teachers; secondary students; professional teams

Separate observation from inference, compare possible explanations and test evidence.

Possible learning outcome: An observation-and-evidence worksheet

Related reading

  • See Before You Explain Learn
  • Observe Before You Explain Journal
Explore the original activity concept: Below the Surface

A fictional doorway scene gives the group a shared starting point. Someone approaches, pauses, looks right and turns back. Each participant sees the same sequence, but explanations quickly multiply. The workshop makes those differences available for discussion.

  • Write an observation that another reader can check.
  • Keep an interpretation provisional and identify missing context.
  • Turn a quick judgement into a fairer next question.
  • Notice the scene individually through words, a drawing or an accessible description.
  • Compare three viewpoints and map what each makes visible.
  • Build a visible / inferred / unknown panel in small groups, with a pause between activities.
  • Use a seated or standing tableau to explore position, then leave with a question map.

These are illustrative planning notes, not a confirmed session format or published timetable.

AET-WS-015Movement, Sport & Coaching

Teaching Skills Through Games

Suitable for Coaches; PE teachers

Change game rules to investigate different movement goals and ways to learn.

Possible learning outcome: A progressive game-design card

Related reading

  • Choosing a Movement Practice Journal
Explore the original activity concept: Changing the Rules

Keep the same people and objects, then change one rule. The task may become quicker, more collaborative or easier for a different person to join. This lab uses the comparison itself as a learning tool.

  • Identify how a rule changes roles, choices and interaction.
  • Design an alternative with a clear purpose.
  • Describe participation without treating a game as a test of a person.
  • Understand a simple shared game before trying a version.
  • Record who can contribute, what the task requires and what remains unclear.
  • Change one rule and offer a seated or lower-intensity way to join.
  • Compare versions and make an adaptation card.

These are illustrative planning notes, not a confirmed session format or published timetable.

AET-WS-019Leadership & Workplaces

Perspective-Taking & Team Communication

Suitable for Managers; staff teams; NGOs

Compare a shared workplace situation from multiple viewpoints before deciding what happened.

Possible learning outcome: A two-perspective communication map

Related reading

  • See Before You Explain Learn
Explore the original activity concept: Behind the Hill

Start with a map that stops at a hill. Each person draws what might be on the other side before comparing answers. The differences become material for a shared investigation rather than a contest to find the most convincing story.

  • Distinguish an incomplete view from an incorrect claim.
  • Compare perspectives without ranking the people who hold them.
  • Identify what new information could change a shared account.
  • Choose a position on an illustrated map and sketch the view.
  • Exchange drawings and name the differences.
  • Use a tabletop screen or room layout to compare lines of sight.
  • Rebuild the map with evidence, possibilities and unanswered questions clearly marked.

These are illustrative planning notes, not a confirmed session format or published timetable.

AET-WS-044Design, Space & Accessibility

Accessible Routes & Public Spaces

Suitable for Municipalities; schools; architects; clubs

Observe routes, crossings and resting points using maps and fictional users.

Possible learning outcome: An annotated accessibility map

Related reading

  • Reading a Place for Movement Journal
  • Lebanon Disability Inclusion Strategy through 2030 News
Explore the original activity concept: Movement, Space & City

Two people can follow the same street and notice different things: a shortcut, a place to rest, a difficult crossing or a welcome meeting point. This workshop reads a place through those experiences and asks which details a simple map leaves out.

  • Map routes, pauses and barriers without using personal addresses.
  • Separate visible features from access assumptions.
  • Develop a question or modest improvement proposal for the people who use the place.
  • Sketch a familiar type of place or use the fictional example.
  • Mark routes, crossings, shade, resting points and boundaries.
  • Compare perspectives using a tabletop route or a pre-checked optional visit.
  • Choose one question to investigate and create an anonymous learning panel.

These are illustrative planning notes, not a confirmed session format or published timetable.

AET-WS-061Culture, History & Storytelling

Collaborative Creativity & Visual Storytelling

Suitable for Youth groups; cultural organisations; workplaces

Build a shared visual narrative with multiple roles and low-pressure participation.

Possible learning outcome: A collaboratively produced visual story

Related reading

  • The Joy of Becoming Exhibition Discover
  • Mare Nostrum: Mediterranean Art Discover
Explore the original activity concept: Soft Social

Conversation is one way to meet; making something can be another. At a shared table, people choose a shape, word or movement mark and gradually build a mural or modular arrangement. A contribution can be quiet, spoken or made alongside someone else.

  • Notice how materials and setup offer different ways to join.
  • Share decisions about a collective object.
  • Describe a participation choice without assigning a motive to someone else.
  • Begin at an open making station without a compulsory introduction.
  • Choose materials and add a first contribution.
  • Arrange, exchange or modify pieces in pairs, independently or silently.
  • Look at the whole work and optionally discuss how it changed.

These are illustrative planning notes, not a confirmed session format or published timetable.
