Observe before you explain

Aetherion editorial · Published 2026-10-28

What the source establishes

This article proposes a way to organise notes. It reports no experiment and claims no validated assessment method. An observation describes something available in a scene; an interpretation proposes what it might mean. In practice, the boundary needs discussion.

Aetherion’s interpretation

Imagine a fictional player who pauses before receiving a pass. “The player stopped for two seconds” describes timing. “The player lacked confidence” assigns an explanation. Other possibilities include watching a teammate, waiting for space or responding to an instruction outside the camera frame. The pause alone cannot decide between them.

Explore the idea

Make two columns: what I noticed and what I think it might mean. Use a fictional scene or footage you have permission to view. Record a time, direction or action without guessing a diagnosis, motive or ability level. Then write two possible explanations and identify what additional context would help distinguish them. Keep uncertainty visible even if one explanation feels obvious.

Questions worth exploring

What happened just before the moment? What does the camera leave out? Could the person describe the situation differently? Which sentence in your notes contains an assumption?

Scope and limitations

This is an editorial note-taking exercise, not a diagnostic, scouting or performance-rating tool. Do not record identifying details about people without their permission.
