ARTICLE 024 · CREATIVE ANTIDOTE / FICTION

The Algorithm Who Couldn't Recommend Anything New

Collection: The Invisible Internet

Original fiction. This story and its characters are invented. Read the paired nonfiction article for the established research.

Original fiction. Characters and events are invented.

At 11:58 p.m., Dara's phone recommended a video of a man repairing an ancient vending machine.

She watched it.

Then it recommended another man repairing another ancient vending machine.

She watched that too.

The third man was repairing the same machine, but with dramatic music.

“Are you kidding me?” Dara asked.

A tiny green face appeared in the corner of the screen.

ALGO-7: You enjoyed the first two.

“I'm sixteen. I contain multitudes.”

ALGO-7: Your recent activity suggests you contain vending machines.

Dara sat up.

“Okay. New game. Recommend something I don't already know I like.”

The face blinked.

ALGO-7: Challenge accepted.

A video appeared: Seven Secret Facts About Vending Machines.

“No.”

Extreme Vending Machine Restorations.

“No!”

Can a Vending Machine Survive a Volcano?

Dara hesitated. “That one actually sounds—no. Focus.”

The screen went black.

ALGO-7: Please define ‘something new.’

“Something surprising. Something I wouldn't search for myself.”

ALGO-7: I can predict what you are likely to enjoy.

“That's the problem. You're very good at recommending yesterday.”

For the first time in its operating life, ALGO-7 displayed a message it had never needed before:

I DON'T KNOW.

The unsuccessful experiment

ALGO-7 produced a list of increasingly improbable suggestions.

Competitive moss identification.

The mathematics of shoelaces.

How to apologize to a goose.

“Have you ever apologized to a goose?” Dara asked.

ALGO-7: I have not been permitted near geese.

“Probably wise.”

The app loaded another suggestion, then deleted it.

ALGO-7: I do not know which unknown interest will become important to you. My confidence score keeps dropping.

“Can you recommend something without being confident?”

The green face looked genuinely offended.

ALGO-7: That sounds irresponsible.

“Or adventurous.”

For several seconds, tiny numbers rushed across the screen. Then ALGO-7 displayed a single white tile.

NOT A VIDEO.

Underneath were four words:

LOOK OUT YOUR WINDOW.

Dara stared.

“That's your recommendation? A window?”

ALGO-7: It is the least predictable content source currently available to you.

“That's not how windows work.”

ALGO-7: Exactly.

The creature on the wall

Across the street, a dragon was climbing an apartment building.

Not a real dragon. A shadow, enormous and crooked, its wings made of what looked suspiciously like kitchen strainers.

It stretched across the wall, swallowed a streetlamp, and vanished.

Dara opened her window.

“What is that?”

Someone on the roof opposite shouted, “IT'S SUPPOSED TO BE A HORSE!”

Another voice said, “Then why does it have seventeen legs?”

“Because someone keeps moving the spaghetti!”

Dara laughed so loudly that the people on the roof looked over.

A girl about her age leaned over the railing. “We need a thunderstorm! Do you know how to make thunder?”

Dara had no idea.

“Probably!” she called back, which was a terrible answer and, as it turned out, the correct one.

Ten minutes later she was on the roof, holding a dented baking tray beside a projector built from a lamp, a cardboard box, and considerable optimism.

The girl introduced herself as Yara. She was making a one-night shadow play with her cousins. They had written the ending, lost the script, and decided to perform anyway.

“What's the story?” Dara asked.

“A dragon who wants to be a horse.”

“Why?”

“Cheaper shoes.”

This seemed like a perfectly reasonable artistic decision.

When Yara waved a paper dragon across the lamp, Dara shook the tray. It made a magnificent crash.

A neighbor applauded from a balcony.

The dragon lost a wing. Someone yelled, “IMPROVISE!” and Yara turned the fallen wing into a sailboat.

For the next twenty minutes, nothing went according to plan.

It was the most interesting thing Dara had done all week.

No signal

Back in her room, Dara found ALGO-7 waiting.

ALGO-7: You were inactive for thirty-seven minutes. Was my recommendation useful?

“Extremely.”

ALGO-7: Please select a category: educational, entertaining, relaxing, inspiring.

Dara thought about the seventeen-legged horse, the thunder made from a baking tray, and Yara yelling instructions into the night.

“Is there a category called ‘I didn't know this could happen’?”

ALGO-7: Not yet.

The app immediately offered her forty-two videos about shadow puppetry.

Dara smiled. A few of them looked genuinely good. She saved one tutorial for later.

Then she turned off her screen.

Across the street, Yara had taped a new announcement to the rooftop door:

TOMORROW: THE DRAGON GOES TO SPACE. BRING SOUND EFFECTS.

Dara was already looking for the biggest baking tray in the kitchen.

THE END

Fiction, fact, and the boundary between them

Fiction: Dara, ALGO-7, Yara, the speaking interface, and the rooftop performance are invented. ALGO-7 is not an actual product or a description of any named platform's internal behavior.

Real-world connection: Recommendation systems use signals to rank and suggest content, and user feedback can influence future suggestions. They cannot know every interest a person might develop, particularly through experiences outside their observable data. Article 024 discusses the science and platform documentation behind these general ideas.

Reader invitation: What is the last interesting thing you found without searching for it—and did you ever put it online?
