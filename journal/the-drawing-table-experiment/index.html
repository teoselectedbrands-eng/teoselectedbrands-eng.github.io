Journal · Field Notes · A short experiment

The drawing didn't change.
The setting did.

A pencil, a sheet of paper and the same person. What happens when we change the conditions around the activity?

Explore three versions ↓

One activity. Three environments.

Imagine trying to draw a simple shape at a table. The drawing task stays the same. In each imagined version, only the surroundings change.

With more light, less noise or a different position for the tools, someone might find the task easier. Someone else may prefer different conditions. The observation matters more than assuming that one arrangement suits everyone.

Person × Activity × Environment

Look for what can change.

When a task feels difficult, we often focus on individual effort. But everyday functioning also depends on the activity itself and the context in which it happens. A small adjustment can be worth exploring before simply asking someone to try harder.

P

Person

Experience, preferences, capacities and what matters to them.

A

Activity

Steps, tools, expectations and time needed.

E

Environment

Light, sound, arrangement, space and other people.

Try this: Before changing the person or the goal, adjust one environmental detail. Ask what changed, what stayed difficult, and whether the person prefers the new setup.

Could this become a group experiment?

A class, workplace or community group could turn this question into a simple learning encounter. It would require an agreed facilitator, audience, accessibility plan and format; no event is scheduled from this article.
