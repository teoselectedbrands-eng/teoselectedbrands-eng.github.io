# Aetherion Movement — Movement Atlas, Learning & Access Architecture
**Private working specification · 15 November 2026 · Do not deploy or activate services**
**Continuity:** PR #17, starting review head `5a6d950e9737b65a08a5890513ce3e6a29603622`. This document is additive. It does not supersede site-specific operational authorisations or create a public offer.

## 1. Governing principle
**Explain the practice first. Teach, demonstrate, adapt, then invite exploration.** Do not open with decontextualised Hang/Squat/Balance/Rotate/Jump/Sprint lists or abstract reflective questions before the reader has a useful model. Do not reduce human functioning to fitness movements. The relationships among person, body, nervous system, task, materials, people, physical environment, intention, access, and participation form the educational frame. The WHO ICF is a cited framework, not an Aetherion invention.

## 2. Editorial contract: every practice guide
Every published method page should eventually contain the following, in this order:
1. **What is this method?** A plain-language definition, history/disciplinary context where relevant, why someone might choose it, and what it does *not* promise.
2. **What do words mean?** A small annotated dictionary: e.g. control = directing and adapting range, timing, force and sequence; posture = arrangement of body segments for a task, not one universally correct static pose; resistance = demand muscles act against; coordination = organising component actions in time and space; rhythm = temporal pattern of events; adaptation = modifying conditions to enable a useful, meaningful task.
3. **What will we actually do?** At least three fully described sample sessions/activities: aim, accessible beginning, preparation/equipment, suggested progression, alternative, role of practitioner, what to observe, when to stop or refer, and what transfer to life could look like. These are teaching examples, not universal clinical prescriptions.
4. **What might be learned?** Distinguish knowledge, motor skill, muscular function, participation and confidence; never imply identical or guaranteed outcomes.
5. **Levels and progression:** the named stages describe learning pathways, not automatic grade labels, age restrictions or promised session counts. Explain readiness criteria; do not invent fixed milestones.
6. **Format and capacity:** 1:1, 1:2, 1:3 are micro-practice streams; a group workshop/event is a separately facilitated format with independent operational capacity and risk controls. A stated format is not bookability.
7. **Illustration/video:** an anatomical/functional diagram, a sequence of real activity, and an adaptation comparison; caption what changes and why. Use approved original brand artwork, and licensed or commissioned educational visuals. Never publish external image-search results by default.
8. **Source and currency:** peer-reviewed/systematic evidence when available; separate studied claims from teaching proposals; credit authors and describe limitations.
9. **Next action:** learn free without personal data, request general information, or access a *real* confirmed offer only when operationally enabled.

## 3. Six methods — teaching content, not just names

### Pilates
Definition: mind-body exercise that emphasises strength, trunk control, flexibility, attention to movement, posture and breathing; mat and equipment variants. Explain the six commonly cited historical principles (centering, concentration, control, precision, flow, breathing) without implying their presence or use is universal across research.
- **Examples:** supported breathing and pelvic orientation; pelvic tilt -> bridge variation; side-lying leg movement; quadruped opposite-limb reach; standing/seated variants; a short coordinated sequence.
- **What is control?** Selecting range, direction, speed, force and breath for the present task, without stiffness or a single ideal shape.
- **What is posture?** An adaptable relation of body segments, support and task; avoid a 'perfect posture' myth or diagnosing by silhouette.
- **Three learning arcs:** discover positions and breath; coordinate components with variable support; apply linked movements to individual goals.
- **Scope:** movement-education Pilates and clinician-led rehabilitation are separate services, even if some techniques overlap.

### Calisthenics
Bodyweight resistance using gravity, leverage, stability, support and movement range. Begin with a chosen task and accessible support, not target repetitions.
- **Examples:** wall -> lower incline pushing; supported rowing; sit-to-stand using different heights; step-ups; assisted trunk support; grip preparation; low-risk walk-carry-reach circuit.
- **Distinct outcomes:** practice can refine skill; sufficient resistance can build force capacity; suitably dosed training may increase muscle size. Neither repetition nor soreness alone proves a particular outcome.
- **Three arcs:** learn supports; progressively adjust mechanical demand and recovery; connect skills to meaningful settings. Practitioner must decide readiness.

### Rhythm, Dance & Coordination
Rhythm means patterns in timing (beat, pause, duration, synchrony, change), not just music. Activities can include stepping, direction change, body percussion, call-and-response, choreography, partner mirroring, karate footwork as a non-contact coordination example, and seated / wheelchair-based alternatives.
- **Three arcs:** recognise patterns; vary, coordinate and respond; create and share an original phrase.
- **Scope:** teach martial arts skills only with an appropriately qualified instructor; cultural forms need context and respectful credit.

### Play, Discovery & Cooperation
Play is a learning process through imagination, social roles, material exploration and rules. Describe actual activities for schools, families and intergenerational contexts.
- **Examples:** cardboard city with negotiated roles; cooperative map and clue trails; shared sound-and-movement story; safe ingredient/utensil sorting or a properly governed food activity; outdoor object transport; small games festival.
- **Three arcs:** introduce setting and choice; experiment and negotiate; create and share something tangible.
- **Gate:** child safeguarding, consent, food/allergen rules when relevant, supervision, capacity, safe materials, accessibility and a named responsible facilitator are prerequisites to a live activity.

### Outdoor Movement
Not simply a gym relocated outside. Programmes combine travel, terrain, materials, weather, people and goals.
- **Examples:** accessible arrival loop; graded walking paths; low-height reach and grip exploration; carry and transport stations; partner route-making; shade, rest and recovery.
- **800–900 m² concept only:** six possible functional zones: accessible circulation + orientation; ground/terrain; reach/grip; carry/build; rhythm/cooperation; rest/shade. None is an approved engineering design. Do not publish a mapped installation as existing. Actual survey, permissions, accessibility, load/rating calculations, fall zones, inspection, maintenance, weather protocol, safeguarding and insurance precede installation or booking.

### Everyday Movement Across Life
Choose tasks by purpose and preference, not by a person's age or appearance. Include adults, older people, families, and participants with varied abilities without assigning everyone to separate 'senior' or 'kids' boxes.
- **Examples:** reach and organise shelf; prepare a simple meal; garden; navigate a public space; change from sitting to standing if meaningful; create a social dance; adapt a work or school task.
- **Three arcs:** identify meaningful participation; adjust one or more conditions; sustain or extend participation. Clinical assessment and therapy should remain a distinct, appropriately regulated professional pathway.

## 4. A concrete beginner Pilates learning sequence (illustrative only)
- **Lesson 1 — Understanding the method:** What Pilates is; support, surface, breathing; demonstrate a comfortable seated or lying position with alternatives.
- **Lesson 2 — Motion and support:** Introduce pelvic tilt as an example of small intentional movement; explain joints and changing relationship to the mat.
- **Lesson 3 — Force and coordination:** Compare a supported bridge variation to a simpler pelvic movement; ask what extra demand changes.
- **Lesson 4 — Linking:** Introduce side-lying or quadruped pattern; compare sequences with adapted range/support.
- **Lesson 5 — Carryover:** Connect coordination to reaching, standing, transitioning, or another chosen activity. Assess learning by a suitable demonstration or explanation, not repetition counts alone.
Actual duration, intensity, restrictions, instructor competence and readiness are determined per programme.

## 5. Observation worksheet: teach first, observe second
The companion printable draft is at `/resources/movement-observation-worksheet/` on this review branch, currently **not in public navigation**.
Suggested fields: goal / activity / setting; relevant person-task-environment conditions; steps actually observed (descriptive, not diagnostic); support, load and pacing; change one condition; response; safety signs; next option or referral. Do not record names, health histories or personal data in the general website. An observation is not a diagnosis or a clinical assessment.

## 6. Pathways, service taxonomy and site architecture
- **Open Learning:** no account, no payment, genuine articles/guides/free samples.
- **Guided Learning:** sequenced modules with outcomes, teaching material, optional coaching, accessible assessment and clear accommodation route; not yet active.
- **Workshops & Practice:** conditional in-person and online delivery, free or fee-bearing as specifically authorised; payment may be offline but only after transparent terms, cancellation/refund policy and confirmations. Do not imply 'free' if programme has a fee.
- **Membership:** optional free identity for persistent bookmarks/progress only when secure registration, privacy, deletion and retention are actually implemented. Do not gate open content.
- **Certificates:** Aetherion participation, completion or assessed achievement are distinct types. An accredited or CE/CPD certificate requires separate relevant institutional/regulatory authorisation; Aetherion-owned branded credentials must not be misrepresented as external accreditation. Include exact course ID/version, assessed requirements, instructor and authorised signatory, issue time, accessible verification, revocation/correction process.
- **Professional services:** therapy / home visits / professional referral / home-based trainer must be distinct from general classes, routed according to real provider competence, scope, privacy and staffing.
- **Future Shop:** separate not-yet-live pathway, no phantom inventory or checkout.

## 7. Inquiry: general help before personal intake
The `/inquiry/` route currently creates a `mailto:` draft with broad, non-sensitive choices; this **is not an inquiry submission system**. Preserve that distinction.
An eventual real inquiry system should use:
1. Category: unsure; practice; workshop; learning; clinical/professional; home support; school/workplace; B2B.
2. Preferred format: guidance; 1:1; 1:2; 1:3; group/event; remote; home (availability not implied).
3. Broad context: self; family; institution; practitioner; unsure. Minimise free text.
4. Explicit **review your choices**, communication consent and a plain-language privacy notice before collection of contact details.
5. Secure server endpoint, anti-abuse controls, no secrets in frontend, operator routing, staff roles, response SLA, audit record, retention and deletion, and a separate protected clinical intake when needed.
6. State transitions: NEW -> TRIAGE -> CLARIFICATION_NEEDED / REFERRED / OFFER_PREPARED -> OWNER_APPROVED_OFFER -> ACCEPTED / CLOSED. A request never equals booking.
7. No unreviewed data sharing with third parties, no patient info through mailto, no child identifiers on a public form.

## 8. Bookings: deliberately separate from inquiry
- Catalogue offer -> confirm actual facilitator, date/time, physical/privacy/acoustic/service/practitioner/transition constraints -> available seat or 1:1 time -> collect minimal contact and required consent -> issue confirmation -> reminder/cancellation rules.
- **Not** enabled by adding a 'Book' button; server-side capacity and calendar synchronisation are required.
- Video meeting links only for confirmed events and authorised attendees; never publish reusable private Zoom URLs.
- For in-person fees paid directly: display approved fees, payment timing, cancellation and receipt policy. Do not activate online card payments without a separate decision.

## 9. Visual and identity rules
Use only the approved original Aetherion Movement logo assets and matching gold-on-forest / green-on-ivory treatments from the Drive toolkit. Preserve shape, arc, dot and three curves. Check actual title spelling on *all* page families before any release. The arbitrary 'A' favicon needs a separate approved source asset, not a guessed redraw.
