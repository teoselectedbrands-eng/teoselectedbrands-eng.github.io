OPEN INTRODUCTION

Give the movement a purpose.

Imagine a tote resting on a shop counter and a table nearby. “Move the tote to the table so the counter is clear” describes an activity with a start, a destination and a reason. “Lift correctly” has not yet told us what is happening.

A task in context

Activity and purpose
Move the tote to clear the counter.
Object
A fictional bag with two handles.
Route
A short path with a turn around a chair.
Time and people
No deadline is specified; someone else shares the space.
Unknown
The description does not establish the bag’s weight, the surface or individual suitability.

Change one condition on paper.

Place the table nearer the counter in a drawing. The route on the plan becomes shorter. Whether the whole task becomes easier for a particular person is still a question; turns, other users and the person’s experience may matter.

Try the same comparison with an invented route to a reading room. Carry forward the useful questions and adapt them to the new task. This is a way to organise inquiry, rather than a physical challenge or a test of skill transfer.
