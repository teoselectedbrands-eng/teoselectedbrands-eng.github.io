OPEN INTRODUCTION

Start with one sentence.

In a fictional scene, a person walks toward a doorway, stops, turns their head right and returns along the corridor. The sequence tells us about actions and order. It does not supply the person’s reason.

Compare two notes

What the scene providesA possible explanation
The person stopped near the doorway.They may have noticed that the room was closed.
The person turned their head right.They may have heard someone or seen a sign.

The second column is useful when its status stays visible. A sign could be relevant, but its presence would not show that the person read it. “I cannot tell from this scene” can be the most accurate conclusion.

A short exploration

Write or say one observation, two possible explanations and one piece of missing context. Use the fictional scene; you do not need to observe a stranger or share a personal story.

Check your reasoning

Which sentence would another reader be able to check from the description alone? Which sentence adds a motive? Keep the distinction on your worksheet rather than trying to select a winning explanation.
