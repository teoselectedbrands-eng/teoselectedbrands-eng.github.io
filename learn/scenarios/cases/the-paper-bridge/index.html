SC-004 · Track 04 · Play & Participation

The rule that silenced a contribution

Does less talking mean less participation?

Fictional scenario / two proposed versions / no real-world outcome verified. This case is a learning companion to a curriculum outline or existing introduction—not a completed or accredited course.

Given in fiction

Stated inside this invented case, not independently observed.

Interpretation

A possible story, not proof of intent or cause.

Proposed change

A redesign to consider; not an implemented outcome.

Evidence needed

The real checks required before claiming results.

01 · Context

What's given in the fictional scene?

Four fictional adults build a bridge from paper. A rule allows one builder to touch materials while others can only give spoken instructions.

  • One participant handles the paper.
  • Two participants speak during the task.
  • One participant draws and speaks very little.

02 · Separate the claim

Don't confuse a story with a fact.

Unsupported interpretation

The quiet participant has no ideas or is disengaged.

What we don't know: Whether the participant chose a drawing route, found spoken turns useful, or wanted to contribute in another way.

Terminology check

A fictional scene gives us described actions or conditions. It cannot establish unseen motives, actual accessibility, participation outcomes or causal effects.

03 · Scenario architecture

Compare two proposed versions.

V1 · Design proposalV1 — Keep speaking-only contributions

Ask everyone except the builder to explain ideas out loud.

Constraint: The rule narrows valid contribution formats and can generate public pressure.

V2 · Revision proposalV2 — Negotiate contribution modes

Offer voluntary building, sketching, feedback and observation, with clear transitions between roles.

Constraint: More options can introduce material crowding or unclear group responsibilities.

Trade-off to investigate: Expanded choice is promising on paper, but does not demonstrate actual belonging or quality of group participation.

04 · Evaluate the design

What evidence would change our decision?

Review criterion

Does the invitation explicitly allow multiple meaningful modes without assigning individuals to them?

Real-world evidence needed: Check the written roles, observe real use only with appropriate safeguards, and seek optional, privacy-respecting feedback.

Reveal a cautious review conclusion

The fictional description can help compare assumptions and alternatives. Version 02 is proposed, not proven better. A real-world decision needs context-specific observation, suitable checks and appropriate safeguards before anyone claims improvement.

05 · Facilitate and iterate

One invitation, one next question.

“You can sketch, build, describe or observe. What does each contribution help the group discover?”

Describe→Question→Compare→Review→Revise

Take away one question that can be checked and one assumption that should remain open. There is no submission, saved response or score.
