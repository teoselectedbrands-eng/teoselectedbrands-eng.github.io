SC-002 · Track 02 · Movement Literacy

A tote, a route, a purpose

When we move the table, what else changes?

Fictional scenario / two proposed versions / no real-world outcome verified. This case is a learning companion to a curriculum outline or existing introduction—not a completed or accredited course.

Given in fiction

Stated inside this invented case, not independently observed.

Interpretation

A possible story, not proof of intent or cause.

Proposed change

A redesign to consider; not an implemented outcome.

Evidence needed

The real checks required before claiming results.

01 · Context

What's given in the fictional scene?

In a fictional shop, a tote sits on a counter and must be moved to a table. A chair and another visitor share the route.

  • The intended goal is to clear the counter.
  • The tote has two handles in the drawing.
  • The path includes a turn around a chair.

02 · Separate the claim

Don't confuse a story with a fact.

Unsupported interpretation

Moving the table closer will definitely make the task easier for everyone.

What we don't know: Tote weight, individual preferences, passage widths, traffic, reach and whether the table blocks other users.

Terminology check

A fictional scene gives us described actions or conditions. It cannot establish unseen motives, actual accessibility, participation outcomes or causal effects.

03 · Scenario architecture

Compare two proposed versions.

V1 · Design proposalV1 — Shorten the path

Move the table next to the counter on the plan.

Constraint: The shorter route might reduce shared passage space.

V2 · Revision proposalV2 — Design for both tasks and circulation

Try a nearby table position while preserving a usable shared route; ask what real measurements are needed.

Constraint: A diagram cannot determine actual access or individual task suitability.

Trade-off to investigate: Distance may improve while crowding or reach gets worse. One variable never explains the whole arrangement.

04 · Evaluate the design

What evidence would change our decision?

Review criterion

What observable information would distinguish a shorter drawn route from a genuinely more usable task?

Real-world evidence needed: Document actual dimensions and seek input from people who use the space before claiming improved access or effort.

Reveal a cautious review conclusion

The fictional description can help compare assumptions and alternatives. Version 02 is proposed, not proven better. A real-world decision needs context-specific observation, suitable checks and appropriate safeguards before anyone claims improvement.

05 · Facilitate and iterate

One invitation, one next question.

“Name the goal first, then the objects, path, time and people. Which condition did we change?”

Describe→Question→Compare→Review→Revise

Take away one question that can be checked and one assumption that should remain open. There is no submission, saved response or score.
