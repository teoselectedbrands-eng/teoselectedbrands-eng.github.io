SC-003 · Track 03 · Place & Environment

Two paths to the reading room

Why might a longer path offer a better choice?

Fictional scenario / two proposed versions / no real-world outcome verified. This case is a learning companion to a curriculum outline or existing introduction—not a completed or accredited course.

Given in fiction

Stated inside this invented case, not independently observed.

Interpretation

A possible story, not proof of intent or cause.

Proposed change

A redesign to consider; not an implemented outcome.

Evidence needed

The real checks required before claiming results.

01 · Context

What's given in the fictional scene?

A fictional library plan shows a direct route through a narrow doorway and a longer route through a shared corridor with unclear signage.

  • Route A is shorter in the sketch.
  • Route A has a visibly marked narrow doorway.
  • Route B takes more turns and passes an unclear sign.

02 · Separate the claim

Don't confuse a story with a fact.

Unsupported interpretation

The longer route is accessible, because it avoids the narrow door.

What we don't know: Actual clear widths, slopes, door operation, surface, rest opportunities, sign visibility and the visitors' experience.

Terminology check

A fictional scene gives us described actions or conditions. It cannot establish unseen motives, actual accessibility, participation outcomes or causal effects.

03 · Scenario architecture

Compare two proposed versions.

V1 · Design proposalV1 — Send everyone along Route B

Place one arrow directing all visitors toward the longer corridor.

Constraint: The corridor signage and route conditions are not verified.

V2 · Revision proposalV2 — Offer informed alternatives

Clarify both options, remove temporary obstructions where feasible and verify real constraints before directing visitors.

Constraint: Improving information does not certify physical usability or building-code compliance.

Trade-off to investigate: A clearer wayfinding system could add clutter; a shorter path could be constrained by its doorway.

04 · Evaluate the design

What evidence would change our decision?

Review criterion

What would we need to measure, observe and ask before promoting either route?

Real-world evidence needed: Conduct a genuine site survey and appropriately consented user review; check applicable local standards separately.

Reveal a cautious review conclusion

The fictional description can help compare assumptions and alternatives. Version 02 is proposed, not proven better. A real-world decision needs context-specific observation, suitable checks and appropriate safeguards before anyone claims improvement.

05 · Facilitate and iterate

One invitation, one next question.

“Our sketch tells us about the drawing. What would we have to inspect in the building?”

Describe→Question→Compare→Review→Revise

Take away one question that can be checked and one assumption that should remain open. There is no submission, saved response or score.
