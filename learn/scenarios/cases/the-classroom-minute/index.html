SC-006 · Track 06 · Families & Schools

Five minutes, several invitations

Does one instruction create the same opportunity for everyone?

Fictional scenario / two proposed versions / no real-world outcome verified. This case is a learning companion to a curriculum outline or existing introduction—not a completed or accredited course.

Given in fiction

Stated inside this invented case, not independently observed.

Interpretation

A possible story, not proof of intent or cause.

Proposed change

A redesign to consider; not an implemented outcome.

Evidence needed

The real checks required before claiming results.

01 · Context

What's given in the fictional scene?

A fictional educator is designing a five-minute movement-and-observation invitation in a room with close desks and different participation preferences.

  • The proposed window is five minutes.
  • Desks restrict shared space in the scenario.
  • The educator wants a predictable closing transition.

02 · Separate the claim

Don't confuse a story with a fact.

Unsupported interpretation

Any child who remains seated is not participating or needs a diagnosis.

What we don't know: Room clearances, student preferences, specific support arrangements, school safeguards and suitability for any real group.

Terminology check

A fictional scene gives us described actions or conditions. It cannot establish unseen motives, actual accessibility, participation outcomes or causal effects.

03 · Scenario architecture

Compare two proposed versions.

V1 · Design proposalV1 — A single required action

Tell the group to stand and imitate the same movements.

Constraint: One required body position excludes alternatives and may draw attention to individuals.

V2 · Revision proposalV2 — Voluntary routes toward one purpose

Offer observation, seated or standing alternatives as appropriate, describe transitions, and close without ranking.

Constraint: Choice needs understandable directions, supervision and local adaptation; no health or attention benefit is established.

Trade-off to investigate: A simple instruction takes less explanation; real choice can take more preparation and must remain genuinely optional.

04 · Evaluate the design

What evidence would change our decision?

Review criterion

Can a teacher identify the task purpose, safe spatial limits, available alternatives and stop cue from the plan?

Real-world evidence needed: Evaluate the written plan and local school/safeguarding requirements; do not collect child identities or claim cognitive effects.

Reveal a cautious review conclusion

The fictional description can help compare assumptions and alternatives. Version 02 is proposed, not proven better. A real-world decision needs context-specific observation, suitable checks and appropriate safeguards before anyone claims improvement.

05 · Facilitate and iterate

One invitation, one next question.

“Which options fit the room? You can participate by describing, observing or choosing an appropriate movement.”

Describe→Question→Compare→Review→Revise

Take away one question that can be checked and one assumption that should remain open. There is no submission, saved response or score.
