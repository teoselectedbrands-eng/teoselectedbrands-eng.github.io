SC-001 · Track 01 · Observation & Interpretation

The doorway that invites a story

What can be described before a motive is assigned?

Fictional scenario / two proposed versions / no real-world outcome verified. This case is a learning companion to a curriculum outline or existing introduction—not a completed or accredited course.

Given in fiction

Stated inside this invented case, not independently observed.

Interpretation

A possible story, not proof of intent or cause.

Proposed change

A redesign to consider; not an implemented outcome.

Evidence needed

The real checks required before claiming results.

01 · Context

What's given in the fictional scene?

A fictional corridor at a shared learning centre. Someone approaches a doorway, pauses, glances to one side and walks back.

  • The person approached a doorway.
  • They paused, looked to one side and walked back.
  • No explanation from the person is included.

02 · Separate the claim

Don't confuse a story with a fact.

Unsupported interpretation

They must have felt unwelcome.

What we don't know: Whether they changed plans, noticed a sign, heard a sound, or simply chose another route.

Terminology check

A fictional scene gives us described actions or conditions. It cannot establish unseen motives, actual accessibility, participation outcomes or causal effects.

03 · Scenario architecture

Compare two proposed versions.

V1 · Design proposalV1 — Explain the behaviour

Begin the discussion by asking participants why the person turned back.

Constraint: Questions about inner states can frame one guess as the answer.

V2 · Revision proposalV2 — Separate observation and explanation

Ask for one camera-checkable statement, two possible hypotheses and a missing-context question.

Constraint: Multiple hypotheses take time; the facilitator must not reward confident mind-reading.

Trade-off to investigate: A careful discussion may move slower than a rapid explanation, but it can preserve uncertainty and reduce unjustified labels.

04 · Evaluate the design

What evidence would change our decision?

Review criterion

Can a reviewer identify the observable statement without treating any proposed motive as a fact?

Real-world evidence needed: Review the wording for clear observed/inferred separation; for a real learning session, gather optional feedback on clarity with appropriate consent.

Reveal a cautious review conclusion

The fictional description can help compare assumptions and alternatives. Version 02 is proposed, not proven better. A real-world decision needs context-specific observation, suitable checks and appropriate safeguards before anyone claims improvement.

05 · Facilitate and iterate

One invitation, one next question.

“Tell me one action a camera could show, then one thing we cannot know yet.”

Describe→Question→Compare→Review→Revise

Take away one question that can be checked and one assumption that should remain open. There is no submission, saved response or score.
