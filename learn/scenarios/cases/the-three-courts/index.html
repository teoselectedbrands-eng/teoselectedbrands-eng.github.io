SC-005 · Track 05 · Sport & Movement Culture

One passing game, three cultures

Can a scoreboard tell us who belongs?

Fictional scenario / two proposed versions / no real-world outcome verified. This case is a learning companion to a curriculum outline or existing introduction—not a completed or accredited course.

Given in fiction

Stated inside this invented case, not independently observed.

Interpretation

A possible story, not proof of intent or cause.

Proposed change

A redesign to consider; not an implemented outcome.

Evidence needed

The real checks required before claiming results.

01 · Context

What's given in the fictional scene?

Four fictional adults pass a ball in a park, during sports-club practice and in an adult learning group.

  • The same passing movement occurs in three story settings.
  • The club can count completed passes.
  • The learning group may change a rule to investigate it.

02 · Separate the claim

Don't confuse a story with a fact.

Unsupported interpretation

A higher pass count shows better inclusion or more enjoyment.

What we don't know: Who felt invited, what participation formats were usable, and how participants interpreted the experience.

Terminology check

A fictional scene gives us described actions or conditions. It cannot establish unseen motives, actual accessibility, participation outcomes or causal effects.

03 · Scenario architecture

Compare two proposed versions.

V1 · Design proposalV1 — Score only completed passes

Use a pass count to describe success across all three settings.

Constraint: A metric about a defined action is not a metric of belonging or experience.

V2 · Revision proposalV2 — Keep the count within its scope

Describe the pass count, ask about roles and space, and add voluntary reflection without ranking individuals.

Constraint: More questions require respectful consent and may not produce a single comparable score.

Trade-off to investigate: A simple number is easy to compare; an honest cultural account is richer but less reducible to a leaderboard.

04 · Evaluate the design

What evidence would change our decision?

Review criterion

Which conclusion is supported by a count, and which requires other evidence?

Real-world evidence needed: Define the scoring method and limits, then seek voluntary feedback if conducting a real programme evaluation.

Reveal a cautious review conclusion

The fictional description can help compare assumptions and alternatives. Version 02 is proposed, not proven better. A real-world decision needs context-specific observation, suitable checks and appropriate safeguards before anyone claims improvement.

05 · Facilitate and iterate

One invitation, one next question.

“What did the score measure? What important question did it leave unanswered?”

Describe→Question→Compare→Review→Revise

Take away one question that can be checked and one assumption that should remain open. There is no submission, saved response or score.
