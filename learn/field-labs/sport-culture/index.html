Track 05 · Sport & Movement Culture · Self-paced scenario

Same game. Different culture.

What changes when the same movement enters a park, a sports club or a classroom? Compare spaces, rules, goals and social roles before assuming that competition defines all sport.

Fictional independent learning sample, approximately 15–20 minutes. The full Sport Beyond Competition course is still a curriculum outline. No enrolment, scoring, certificate, health assessment or data submission.

  1. 01 Describe the activity
  2. 02 Compare the settings
  3. 03 Question the outcome
  4. 04 Design a new invitation

01 / Describe

Start with a ball and four people.

Imagine four fictional adults passing a soft ball in a square. Each person may send it to another participant. We have described an object, several actions and a social arrangement. We have not established whether this is a sport, a lesson, a game, a warm-up, or a performance.

What is observable?

Passing, catching and movement

Four participants exchange one ball. The same actions could take place with different intentions and rules.

What needs context?

Meaning, status and purpose

Is the aim to score, cooperate, practise a skill, create a rhythm, entertain others, or simply meet people?

One question to start

How would you describe the activity without using the words “winning”, “team” or “sport”? Which detail would you ask the group to explain?

Reveal one descriptive example

“Four people stand in a square and pass a ball to one another. They sometimes change positions.” This describes an activity; it does not infer why people joined or what they valued.

02 / Compare

One activity across three different settings.

🌳 Public park

Informal invitation

Participation is negotiated among friends. There is no fixed score and people can enter or leave as agreed.

Open question: Who feels welcome, and who decides the rules?

🏟️ Sports club

Structured practice

A facilitator defines turns and goals, and the group may count successful passes. Equipment and times are organised.

Open question: Does scoring change attention or collaboration?

🏫 Learning group

Shared discovery

The group changes one rule, compares what happened, and explains differences without ranking individual participants.

Open question: What did the rule make possible?

Why the same physical movement can mean different things

Actions alone do not define a culture. Rules, institutions, public access, relationships, identities and shared meanings influence how an activity is understood. These fictional descriptions give us questions, not data about actual groups.

03 / Investigate

What would a scoreboard miss?

A hypothetical club counts 20 successful passes. That is a quantity about the activity under a particular rule. It tells us nothing by itself about belonging, satisfaction, accessibility, safety or the reasons someone kept participating.

Measured event

Twenty completed passes in one fictional round.

Possible interpretation

The group coordinated well under these conditions.

Unknown meaning

How participants experienced the round, their choices and who may have been excluded.

Evidence boundary

A count is not an explanation. A statistic needs a definition, a measurement method and a context. It cannot replace asking the people involved what mattered to them.

Read a cautious analysis of the score

“Twenty passes were counted according to the fictional game's rules. To understand participation, we would also need information about who could take part, what options existed and how the group understood the experience.”

04 / Design

Keep the play; change the invitation.

Suppose an organiser wants a community activity where participation is possible through different roles. Propose several voluntary contributions, rather than assuming everyone must pass the ball.

Pass or receiveShape the rulesObserve patternsFacilitate turnsOffer feedback
Your field design

Write a two-sentence invitation for a fictional community group. State one possible benefit, one possible unintended effect, and one observation that would help you evaluate the experience. Different participation roles should be real choices, not labels imposed on individuals.

Propose

Offer several ways to participate.

Observe

What roles are actually usable and voluntarily chosen?

Revise

Ask what to change next time without turning feedback into a ranking.

This is an original educational scenario, not a physical activity prescription, evidence of sport outcomes or confirmation that any club or event exists.
