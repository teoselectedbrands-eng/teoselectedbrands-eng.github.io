Mare Nostrum: Seeing the Mediterranean Beyond Borders

Beirut · Published 6 November 2026 · Source check: 6 November 2026

The Mediterranean is often represented as a familiar blue space surrounded by countries, cities and coastlines. What happens when we look at the sea not as a boundary between places, but as something continuously moving between them?

A Mediterranean in motion

That question is at the heart of Mare Nostrum, an exhibition by artist and researcher Lily Abichahine, curated by Yasmine Helou at Beirut’s Sursock Museum. The project follows Mediterranean waters, histories and ideas across Marseille, İzmir, Palermo and Beirut.

Abichahine’s artistic research brings geography, legal concepts and familiar stories about the sea into conversation. The exhibition is a chance to consider how borders are drawn over waters that continue to circulate.

Why explore it?

This is an invitation to approach the sea through art, migration, ecology, memory and cultural identity. Visitors do not need specialist knowledge to begin asking what the Mediterranean connects, and what people have chosen to divide.

Visit information

The exhibition is scheduled for 6 November 2026 to 21 February 2027 in the Twin Galleries on the ground floor. The museum advertises an opening at 6:00 PM on Friday 6 November. Check admission and hours with the museum before visiting.
