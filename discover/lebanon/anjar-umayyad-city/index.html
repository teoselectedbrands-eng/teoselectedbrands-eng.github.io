Anjar: Reading the Plan of an Eighth-Century City

Editorial publication: 6 November 2026 · Sources checked: 6 November 2026 · UNESCO inscription: 1984

Cities reveal ideas through their streets and buildings. In the Bekaa Valley, Anjar shows how city planning itself can become an archaeological source.

What the source establishes

UNESCO describes Anjar as an early eighth-century Umayyad city founded under Caliph Walid I. Its excavated rectangular layout contains walls, towers, intersecting axes, palaces and baths, giving an unusually clear picture of the settlement's organisation.

Why it is worth discovering

Instead of asking only how old the ruins are, readers can ask why routes divide space as they do and what public and private buildings reveal about movement, commerce and social life.

What to explore or verify next

Use the official UNESCO description to understand the plan before any visit. Opening conditions, conservation areas and access routes require up-to-date confirmation with local authorities.

Source dates and limitations

This is a heritage explainer, not a current access or safety confirmation. The UNESCO listing is historical, not a recent inscription.
