Man'ouché Is More Than Breakfast: A Lebanese Culinary Practice

Editorial publication: 6 November 2026 · Sources checked: 6 November 2026 · UNESCO inscription: 2023

Sometimes a food becomes heritage not because of a single famous recipe, but because of the knowledge, gestures and shared moments involved in making it.

What the source establishes

UNESCO inscribed al-Man'ouché, an emblematic culinary practice in Lebanon, on the Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity in 2023. The dossier describes how dough, toppings, family transmission and neighbourhood bakeries are bound to social life.

Why it is worth discovering

The practice creates opportunities to explore informal learning, work passed between generations and the role of familiar food in daily gatherings. It also shows why culinary heritage is about people and techniques, not just objects.

Look more closely: Heritage is a living practice

UNESCO's inscription concerns a practice passed between people, not the declaration of one recipe as the only authentic version. A bakery can also be a place of learning, exchange and neighbourhood routines. That makes gestures, relationships and variations worth exploring alongside ingredients.

Intangible heritage becomes easier to understand through three layers: the technique of preparation, the way people transmit it, and its meaning in daily life. Recording differences between practices can be more informative than demanding a single standard. Ask permission before recording people while they work.

Try these questions

  1. Ask where a technique was learned; do not assume it came from a class.
  2. Note what a written recipe cannot teach without practice.
  3. Compare a factual culinary essay with its explicitly fictional companion.

What to explore or verify next

Try asking where a baker learned the method, how ingredients or techniques vary, and what a morning bakery contributes to a neighbourhood. These are questions for observation, not a ranked list of restaurants.

Source dates and limitations

Based on UNESCO's heritage description. This is not a restaurant review, culinary certification or claim that every bakery makes the same product.
