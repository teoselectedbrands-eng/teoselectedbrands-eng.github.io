Assistive Products Are About Participation, Not Just Devices

Editorial publication: 6 November 2026 · Sources checked: 6 November 2026 · Evergreen service profile

An assistive product is useful when it fits what a person wants and needs to do. The device alone is not the whole story.

What the source establishes

Arcenciel's Assistive Products Unit describes local production and adaptation of devices, sourcing of specialised technologies, maintenance and repair. Examples listed include mobility supports, communication technologies and software supporting access.

Why it is worth discovering

This provides a practical introduction to the connection between design, daily participation and environmental accessibility. Product provision, individual assessment, fitting and maintenance are distinct parts of a support process.

Look more closely: The person–activity–environment connection

Arcenciel describes both locally manufactured or adapted assistive devices and specialised technologies sourced elsewhere. Its service description includes maintenance and repair. This matters because a device's usefulness depends on what the person wants to do, where it will be used, and whether follow-up support is accessible.

A practical way to understand assistive products is to separate four questions: What activity matters to the person? What creates a barrier? Which features may support participation? Who will arrange assessment, fitting, training and maintenance? Those are prompts for informed choice and qualified assessment—not a recommendation of any device for an individual.

Try these questions

  1. Describe an activity and a barrier before naming a device.
  2. Compare a mobility aid, a communication technology and an environmental modification.
  3. Ask about fitting, user feedback, repair and follow-up after provision.
