Learning to Notice Wildlife: A Youth Visit to Ammiq Wetland

Bekaa · Published 6 November 2026 · Source check: 6 November 2026

On 17 October 2026, young participants from Hima Anjar visited Ammiq Wetland in the Bekaa for a guided educational activity about birds and the habitats on which they depend.

Learning outdoors

The Society for the Protection of Nature in Lebanon (SPNL) published an account of the visit on 25 October. The two-and-a-half-hour activity was part of its PROZHUM project, funded by the French Development Agency (AFD).

Participants explored wetland wildlife with an avifauna expert, learning about birds, their ecological roles and the importance of protecting the habitats on which they depend.

Observation as a starting point

A wetland can become a learning environment in which questions arise directly from observation: Why do certain birds live here? How does water availability change wildlife? What can human activity affect?

Such experiences bring biology, geography and environmental responsibility into a shared context rather than treating them as separate topics.

What the report does and does not show

SPNL’s account establishes that the activity took place and describes its content. It does not independently measure long-term changes in participants’ knowledge, attitudes or conservation behaviour. Those outcomes would need a separate evaluation.

Source dates and limitations

Organiser-reported activity, 17 October 2026. Aetherion did not attend or measure outcomes. Source checked 6 November 2026.
