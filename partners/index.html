SCHOOLS / CLUBS / COMMUNITY ORGANISATIONS

BUILD FROM
A REAL QUESTION.

A useful partnership starts with a group, a setting and something it wants to explore. Aetherion’s programme ideas can support that conversation before anyone announces a pilot.

Three possible starting points.

SCHOOL TEAMS

A classroom activity with a purpose.

Educators could compare a transition in the school day, develop a short activity and adapt its instructions, space and participation choices. The result would be a plan for the school to review.

COMMUNITY GROUPS

A place read together.

A tabletop map or a separately checked route can invite questions about crossings, rest, meeting points and the different ways a place is used.

FACILITATORS

A shared approach to observation.

Fictional scenes let a group examine descriptions and explanations without asking for personal disclosure. A team could use the result to clarify the questions it wants to ask.

From an idea to an agreed pilot.

  1. Describe the learning situation.

    Name the adult contact, intended age range, approximate group size, available setting and the question the team wants to explore. Initial enquiries need no student names or diagnoses.

  2. Agree the scope.

    Choose a programme, its purpose and a possible format. Confirm who would facilitate, host, supervise and decide whether the material suits the group.

  3. Review the practical plan.

    Materials, access alternatives, safeguarding, permissions and information handling need named responsibilities. A proposed facilitator review at least two days before a first delivery is part of the planning model.

  4. Confirm, deliver and reflect.

    Only an agreed occurrence would receive a date and participation details. A recap or continued collaboration would follow a separate decision about what can be shared.

These are partnership proposals. No school, organisation, facilitator or agreement is confirmed here.
