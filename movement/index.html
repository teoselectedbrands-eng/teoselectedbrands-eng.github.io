A useful starting explanation

The body moves. The environment responds.

For a movement to happen, the nervous system coordinates action, muscles generate and control forces, joints permit motion within their available range, and the body interacts with support surfaces, objects, gravity and other people. Attention, learning, emotion and purpose also shape the experience.

Within the person

Body and nervous system

Sensation, joint range, force, coordination, recovery, confidence and motivation all influence what a task asks of the person.

The activity

What are we trying to do?

Walk across the room, hang from a safe support, move a chair, dance with someone or carry an object: these tasks are not interchangeable just because some joints move similarly.

Around us

The living field

Ground, light, obstacles, distance, heat, music, instructions, tools, support, social roles and accessibility change the available actions.

What this means: A chair-rise, squat exercise, loaded lift and dance sequence may share components, but each has a different task, load, intention and environment. Similar movement patterns do not make them the same activity.

Training method · The difference between learning and adaptation

Why repeat? What counts as strength?

Repetition can teach coordination.

Practice can improve the ability to organise a skill, especially when the challenge, instruction and context are appropriate. The same movement is not always the best practice condition for transfer to daily life.

Progressive resistance can build strength.

Muscle strength and size can change with resistance training, depending on challenge, weekly volume, repetition, recovery and the individual's aims. Muscles need not be trained to complete exhaustion for benefits.

Joint movement is a coordinated action.

Joints, muscles, tendons and nervous-system control work together. Movement instructions should consider load, range, support and context instead of promising one ideal posture for everyone.

More is not automatically better.

Task quality, learning opportunities, comfort, recovery and actual purpose matter. Pain, dizziness, or unusual symptoms require stopping and appropriate assessment—not pushing through.

Participation and learning

Different capacities. Same right to explore.

We do not put people into fixed programmes according to age, hair colour or assumed physical ability. A child, an adult or an older person may explore the same activity, with differences in the task, tools, pace, access, roles, supervision and personal goals.
