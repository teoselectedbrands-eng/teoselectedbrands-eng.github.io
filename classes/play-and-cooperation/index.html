Practice method · Educational programme concept

Play, Discovery & Cooperation

Activity-led learning for children, families and mixed-age groups: use materials, imaginary roles, rules, movement and shared problem-solving rather than a rigid exercise script.

Not currently a bookable class

What this practice means

Play gives participants opportunities to investigate objects, negotiate roles, practice movement and create meaning together. Activities can be structured or child-led; meaningful participation matters more than a single performance target.

Practitioner note: Activities need appropriate safeguarding, supervision, consent, accessible space and material safety, especially for children. Participation options should not be assigned solely by age or appearance.

Exercises, activities & materials

These are examples of what a qualified facilitator might develop into a programme. They are not complete instructions or a self-treatment plan.

Indoor

Build a shared city

Use blocks, cardboard and pretend community roles. Explore planning, turn-taking and rearranging the environment.

Outdoor

Object trail

Move light materials between stations using agreed routes and roles, with accessible alternatives.

Creative

Sound and movement story

Build a story with footsteps, claps, gestures or assistive communication.

Daily life

Kitchen exploration without cooking risk

Sort safe utensils and ingredients; explore sequences, culture, sensory choice and roles without collecting allergies or other health details online.

Community

Collaborative treasure map

Teams design a simple map, exchange clues, adjust rules and share decisions.

Family

Festival of small games

Rotate between movement, building and imaginary play with clear options to rest, watch or take a different role.

How learning may progress

Three educational phases—not guaranteed competency levels or a fixed number of sessions.

Invitation

Present the purpose, activity choices, materials, boundaries and opportunities for child-led exploration.

Experiment together

Adapt roles, tools, rules and space to support consent, agency and cooperation.

Share & carry forward

Create something tangible—a map, story, game or object—and identify ways to use it again.

How sessions could be organised

Individual

1:1

Private teaching or assessment when suitable. Planned around one participant's aims.

Partner

1:2

Two participants with compatible goals; individual attention is still available.

Micro-group

1:3

Small facilitated format (maximum three participants), subject to service, privacy and setting suitability.

Group event

Separate programme

Larger group workshops, family sessions, outdoor events and demonstrations have their own facilitators, capacity and safety plan.

Availability, schedule, personnel and fees are not yet announced. Progression is based on readiness and goals, not an automatic move from 1:1 to larger groups.
