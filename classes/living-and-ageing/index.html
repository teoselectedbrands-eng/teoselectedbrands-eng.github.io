Practice method · Educational programme concept

Everyday Movement Across Life

Learning opportunities that can be adapted to the person, interests, setting and activity—not narrow age categories or assumptions about ability.

Not currently a bookable class

What this practice means

Everyday functioning brings movement into home routines, public life, leisure, caring and learning. The same activity can remain meaningful at different stages of life when materials, pace, task demands and environment are adjusted.

Practitioner note: The WHO recommends multicomponent balance and strength activities for older adults, adjusted to functional ability. This is not an assumption that every older adult has impaired mobility: provision should remain individually relevant.

Exercises, activities & materials

These are examples of what a qualified facilitator might develop into a programme. They are not complete instructions or a self-treatment plan.

Home

Reach, organise and prepare

Compare tool placement, support, visibility, pacing and meaningful roles in home tasks.

Mobility

Walking and changing position

Explore routes, transitions, accessible equipment and the context of daily movement.

Community

Garden or public-space activity

Adapt participation through roles such as planting, planning, communicating or moving materials.

Social

Story or dance circle

Invite shared rhythm, memory, expression and differing ways to join.

Task design

Self-directed routine

Choose a personally meaningful aim instead of assuming goals from age.

Professional

Adaptation and assessment

Occupational therapy or physiotherapy assessment is a distinct service when clinical needs are present.

How learning may progress

Three educational phases—not guaranteed competency levels or a fixed number of sessions.

Meaning and preference

Start with what the person wants to do and the resources available.

Adjust one condition

Review access, tools, pace or social arrangements with appropriate support.

Sustain participation

Plan opportunities for independent or supported practice, with referral when clinical review is indicated.

How sessions could be organised

Individual

1:1

Private teaching or assessment when suitable. Planned around one participant's aims.

Partner

1:2

Two participants with compatible goals; individual attention is still available.

Micro-group

1:3

Small facilitated format (maximum three participants), subject to service, privacy and setting suitability.

Group event

Separate programme

Larger group workshops, family sessions, outdoor events and demonstrations have their own facilitators, capacity and safety plan.

Availability, schedule, personnel and fees are not yet announced. Progression is based on readiness and goals, not an automatic move from 1:1 to larger groups.
