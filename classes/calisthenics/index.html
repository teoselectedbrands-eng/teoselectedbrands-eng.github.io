Practice method · Educational programme concept

Calisthenics: Strength through Bodyweight

Use bodyweight, gravity, leverage and support to build movement skills and physical capacity. Start with suitable tasks rather than chasing a fixed number of repetitions.

Not currently a bookable class

What this practice means

Calisthenics means using the body as the primary source of resistance. A floor, wall, bar, bench or support changes leverage and difficulty. Strength development depends on adequate progressively managed challenge and recovery; repetition also refines the way a skill is organised.

Practitioner note: A progression is not a promise of a fixed outcome and does not require maximal repetitions. ACSM 2026 distinguishes strength, muscle growth and power, which respond to different training variables. Exercise to failure is not required for general benefits.

Exercises, activities & materials

These are examples of what a qualified facilitator might develop into a programme. They are not complete instructions or a self-treatment plan.

Push

Wall or incline push pattern

Choose a wall or raised support before progressing to a lower surface, if appropriate.

Pull

Supported row pattern

A bar or suitably rated low station can offer a pulling challenge; grip and station strength require review.

Legs

Sit-to-stand and step-up patterns

Adapt seat height, step height, hand support and movement goals.

Trunk

Ground-based support and hold

Explore trunk support and control without imposing a fixed time target.

Grip

Supported hang preparation

Use low-height, partial-loading or alternative grip tasks; suspended work requires qualified supervision.

Outdoor

Walk–carry–reach circuit

Link tasks across stations and terrains with space, route and rest planning.

How learning may progress

Three educational phases—not guaranteed competency levels or a fixed number of sessions.

Foundation

Map current capacity, access and movement confidence. Start with manageable leverage and support.

Progressive challenge

Progress resistance, range, coordination or load when appropriate; monitor recovery and technique.

Transfer & variation

Combine movements in real outdoor or everyday tasks; maintain safety and context-specific progression.

How sessions could be organised

Individual

1:1

Private teaching or assessment when suitable. Planned around one participant's aims.

Partner

1:2

Two participants with compatible goals; individual attention is still available.

Micro-group

1:3

Small facilitated format (maximum three participants), subject to service, privacy and setting suitability.

Group event

Separate programme

Larger group workshops, family sessions, outdoor events and demonstrations have their own facilitators, capacity and safety plan.
