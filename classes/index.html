PRACTICE / CLASSES & FORMATS

FIND A WAY
IN.

A class name is only a starting point. Explore the experience behind it: the task, the setting, the teaching and the choices available to a learner.

Eleven ways to explore.

These are programme concepts, including the six formats already introduced by Aetherion. No sessions, instructors, dates or bookings are confirmed. The descriptions explain possibilities you can compare.

11 example formats shown

FORMAT IN DEVELOPMENT

Pilates & Control

Breathing, a change of position and a short sequence give this format its structure. A learner can compare what happens when a task is slower, supported or broken into smaller parts.

Beginner · Adult learning

Inside this format

What you could learn

Describe the purpose of a sequence and ask an instructor about pacing, support and alternatives.

A possible experience

A mat-based introduction with seated or supported choices would need an instructor and a suitable space before it could be offered.

FORMAT IN DEVELOPMENT

Calisthenics Foundations

A wall, a chair and the floor offer different ways to organise bodyweight practice. The subject is how support and leverage change the task, rather than a challenge to complete a fixed number of repetitions.

Beginner · Adult learning

Inside this format

What you could learn

Compare two setups, identify their supports and explain what information is needed before trying an unfamiliar version.

A possible experience

Introductory group teaching could use demonstrations, pauses and guided practice. Equipment and supervision would be checked for an actual session.

Follow this learning connection ↗

FORMAT IN DEVELOPMENT

Rhythm & Movement

Begin with a short phrase: step, pause, turn, repeat. Change the timing, direction or relationship with another dancer and the same sequence becomes a different experience.

All levels · Adult learning

Inside this format

What you could learn

Recognise a phrase, describe its timing and explore an expressive variation without needing to perform for an audience.

A possible experience

A studio group or a quieter small-group session could use musical and non-musical cues, with observation as another way to join.

Follow this learning connection ↗

FORMAT IN DEVELOPMENT

Mobility Explorer

Reaching for an object, turning toward a conversation and changing position all have a purpose. This study starts with those ordinary situations and compares the options the task and environment provide.

Beginner · Adult learning

Inside this format

What you could learn

Name the activity, its goal and one condition that could change how it unfolds.

A possible experience

A short guided introduction could combine discussion, drawings and optional movement. Its scope is general learning, with no treatment programme attached.

Follow this learning connection ↗

FORMAT IN DEVELOPMENT

Play & Cooperation

A group has one shared problem: move an object between marked places while leaving everyone a useful role. Changing a rule can change who contributes and what cooperation requires.

All levels · Adult learning

Inside this format

What you could learn

Identify how rules distribute roles and create one alternative that allows a different way to participate.

A possible experience

An adult group concept using light objects, tabletop and seated options. A separate family or school edition would need an age-specific plan.

Follow this learning connection ↗

FORMAT IN DEVELOPMENT

Strength for Daily Life

A carry, a lift and a supported position connect strength with what someone is trying to accomplish. Compare the object, its position and the route before deciding which practice resembles the task.

Beginner to intermediate · Adult learning

Inside this format

What you could learn

Describe a task demand and ask how a proposed exercise relates to it, without reducing capability to a single score.

A possible experience

A small-group learning format could combine demonstrations with discussion of everyday tasks. Loads and individual participation would be agreed with the instructor.

Follow this learning connection ↗

FORMAT IN DEVELOPMENT

Moving Together

Mirroring a gesture, sharing a tempo and leaving space for another person make coordination a shared activity. Partners can collaborate without touch or compulsory introductions.

All levels · Adult learning

Inside this format

What you could learn

Notice timing and distance, communicate a preference and compare individual and shared versions of a phrase.

A possible experience

A low-pressure adult group with spoken, visual and silent prompts. People could join by moving, observing or suggesting a variation.

Follow this learning connection ↗

FORMAT IN DEVELOPMENT

Outdoor Movement Atlas

A route has turns, crossings, surfaces and places to pause. This concept connects movement with reading a place, using a fictional map first and a checked local route only in a confirmed edition.

Introductory · Adult learning

Inside this format

What you could learn

Make a simple map of opportunities, barriers and missing information from a place or drawing.

A possible experience

A tabletop introduction or an optional guided walk. Any outdoor version requires a confirmed route, permissions, weather plan and access information.

Follow this learning connection ↗

FORMAT IN DEVELOPMENT

Balance & Play

Balance changes with the purpose of an action, its available support and what comes next. A playful rule can make the task about reaching, orienting or continuing a sequence rather than remaining perfectly still.

All levels · Adult learning

Inside this format

What you could learn

Describe which condition changed and distinguish visible adjustments from an explanation of why they occurred.

A possible experience

An adult learning concept using adjustable rules, observation and optional supported tasks. It is not a balance assessment.

Follow this learning connection ↗

FORMAT IN DEVELOPMENT

Movement Stories for Families

A fictional journey gives a family a reason to choose a path, invent a rule or help a character. The activity can include storytelling, drawing and movement, with an adult arranging the setting.

Family with adult caregivers · Family concept

Inside this format

What you could learn

Create a shared story with more than one way to take part and ask which version the group wants to revisit.

A possible experience

A family-specific concept. Age range, guardian involvement, supervision and materials would be agreed before any session is announced.

Follow this learning connection ↗

FORMAT IN DEVELOPMENT

Sport Skills Lab

A short acceleration in a game and a straight sprint ask different questions. Start with a video sequence, identify the goal, then compare timing, space and available choices.

Some experience · Adult learning

Inside this format

A learning sequence

Observe an action, describe its sporting purpose and compare a second context. Facilitated practice would be considered only within a confirmed sport-specific session.

Ask about the actual session.

When a real provider offers a class, ask what happens, who leads it, how participation can change and what the terms include. A quiet technical practice and an energetic group session can serve different interests.
