Movement in context.

Observe an activity, its purpose and the surrounding conditions. This is a note-taking tool, not an exercise prescription or individual assessment.

First, understand what you are observing.

Movement never happens in isolation. Imagine carrying a light book from one table to another. The body manages grip, posture, steps and attention. The task has a purpose and an object. The room adds distance, light, support surfaces, obstacles and other people. Changing the table height or choosing a clear path changes the task itself.

This worksheet helps you describe the activity, materials, available supports and the setting. You do not need to measure or diagnose anyone. The examples below are for learning about everyday context, not prescribing an exercise or assessing a person.
