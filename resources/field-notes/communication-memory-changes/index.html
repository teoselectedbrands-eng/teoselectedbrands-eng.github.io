Communication & Memory Changes

Support understanding without talking over the person.

Related workshop topic: Communication & Memory Changes · Sources checked: 6 November 2026 · Original educational resource

One idea to carry

Communication changes may call for more time, fewer distractions and respectful listening. Neither a brief observation nor a workshop can determine whether someone has dementia or another condition.

Try a fictional scenario

In an invented family conversation, a person pauses while looking for a word. A relative rushes to finish the sentence, while another waits and asks whether help would be welcome.

Use this three-step activity

  1. Describe the two communication approaches without assuming a diagnosis.
  2. Rewrite an impatient response as a calm, respectful question that allows time.
  3. Discuss options such as clearer phrasing, reduced background noise and confirming preferences.

Questions worth keeping

  • How can family members preserve the person’s voice in decisions?
  • What changes when an environment is quieter or less hurried?

Evidence and limits

WHO and the UK NHS discuss respectful communication and caregiver support for people living with dementia; the approach may be adapted but is not a diagnosis.

Persistent or sudden changes in thinking or communication need professional evaluation. Sudden neurological symptoms may require emergency attention.
