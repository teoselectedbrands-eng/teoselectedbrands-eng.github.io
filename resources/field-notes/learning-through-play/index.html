Learning Through Play

Make room for curiosity, choices and experimentation.

Related workshop topic: Learning Through Play · Sources checked: 6 November 2026 · Original educational resource

One idea to carry

A child can explore an idea through pretend play, building, drawing or shared problem-solving. Different play formats offer different opportunities; none is a test of a child’s abilities.

Try a fictional scenario

A small group is given paper squares and wooden blocks. Some children build towers; others invent a shop; another child watches and describes what they notice. An adult asks, “How could we make room for each idea?”

Use this three-step activity

  1. Name one learning question, such as “What helps a bridge stay up?”
  2. Offer at least two ways to participate: build, describe, arrange or observe.
  3. Ask what the group noticed and what they might try differently next time.

Questions worth keeping

  • Does every participant need to produce the same object to learn from the activity?
  • What might you change if the room or materials were different?

Evidence and limits

UNICEF describes play and responsive communication as opportunities for learning; the proposed activity is an original illustration, not an outcome-tested programme.

This is general educational material. Adapt to age, context, interests and supervision; it is not a developmental assessment.
