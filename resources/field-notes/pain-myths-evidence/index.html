Pain: Questions, Evidence & Everyday Life

Pain is real, personal and more complex than a single explanation.

Related workshop topic: Pain: Myths, Evidence & Everyday Life · Sources checked: 6 November 2026 · Original educational resource

One idea to carry

Pain and tissue damage are not identical measures. Pain is a personal experience influenced by biological, psychological and social factors; quick one-size-fits-all explanations are often misleading.

Try a fictional scenario

A fictional online post claims: “Everyone with back pain needs the same exercise.” A reader sees many likes and a confident-looking presenter, but no information about whom the advice applies to.

Use this three-step activity

  1. Identify the exact claim and whether it applies to everyone or to a specific situation.
  2. Look for the source, its limits and whether it describes an individual assessment or general education.
  3. Rewrite the claim as a question that could be discussed with a licensed professional.

Questions worth keeping

  • What can a study tell us about a group that it cannot determine for one person?
  • How does respectful language change a conversation about pain?

Evidence and limits

The International Association for the Study of Pain revised its definition in 2020 to recognise the multifaceted, personal nature of pain.

Do not use the activity to diagnose pain, recommend treatment or judge another person’s report. New or concerning symptoms need individual professional advice.
