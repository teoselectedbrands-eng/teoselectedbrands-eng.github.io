Movement Atlas · Practical tool 001

Movement observation in context

First explain the activity. Then invite the person to try a version that suits them. Observe what actually happens, change one condition, and compare. This is a teaching and learning worksheet—not a test of the person.

Privacy and safety. No name, diagnosis, medical record, child identity, address or other sensitive details should be entered. Everything typed remains in this page only; it is not transmitted or saved. Do not use it as a clinical record, safety clearance or medical assessment.

A worked example

Activity: move three light objects from a shelf to a table. Purpose: examine how tool location, available support and the task goal shape participation.

First version

The objects are relatively far away; the route includes a tight corner. The participant chooses how to approach. Observe distance, route, reaching, pauses and voluntary requests for support.

Changed version

Move the shelf closer and clear the corner, while leaving object weight and goal unchanged. Compare the strategy and ease of participation. A different result does not necessarily mean a change in intrinsic strength.

Educational example only: actual loads, routes, environment and supervision need review before use.

How this differs from a clinical assessment

A general movement observation documents an activity and its conditions for educational discussion. A clinical evaluation uses appropriate professional judgement, assessment tools, consent, health context, records and confidentiality obligations. This worksheet does not substitute for those duties.

Learning transfer: the same observation structure can accompany Pilates, bodyweight practice, partner rhythms, children's cooperative games, outdoor routes or daily activities—provided the activity and setting are appropriate.
